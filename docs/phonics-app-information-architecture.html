Phonics App — Information Architecture & Feature Specification

Based on the UK Letters and Sounds framework (DfES 2007), designed for children aged 4–7 and their parents, with a particular focus on parents for whom English is a second language.

1. Goals & Design Principles

Primary goals

  1. Guide children through all 6 phases of the Letters and Sounds programme at their own pace.
  2. Equip non-native English speaking parents to actively support their child's reading journey — even if they are uncertain about English pronunciation themselves.
  3. Make structured phonics approachable: parents should understand the method, not just watch their child use an app.

Design principles

  • Two audiences, one screen. Parent guidance lives in the same view as the child's content — always accessible, never requiring a mode switch or a separate app section.
  • Audio first. Correct pronunciation is the single most important thing a non-native parent needs. Every phoneme, word and example has high-quality audio the parent can listen to independently before teaching.
  • Progress over perfection. Children move through phases when ready, but the app never blocks progress — parents decide when to advance.
  • Accessible language. All parent-facing copy is written in plain English. Technical terms (phoneme, grapheme, digraph) are always explained in context the first time they appear.

2. User Types

User Primary need
Child (4–7) Fun, guided learning with sounds, words and games — large touch targets, animations, audio
Parent (any level) Understand the method, model correct sounds, track progress — guidance always at hand without switching modes

There is no mode toggle. Parent guidance is embedded directly into the app's screens via two persistent UI patterns:

Briefing card — appears near the top of every phase and lesson view. Open by default on first visit so new users immediately understand what the screen covers. Collapses to a single tappable row on return visits so experienced users reach their lessons without scrolling past content they've already read. Contains: plain-language overview, audio for all sounds in the phase, and a link to full home practice tips.

Floating guide button (📖) — used during active lesson flow, where a briefing card would compete with the child's learning content. A small button in the corner opens a bottom sheet without navigating away from the lesson. Contains: what this step is teaching, pronunciation notes, and an exact script the parent can say aloud.

In practice, the expected session pattern is: parent opens a phase → reads the briefing card → listens to the sounds → both sit down together → parent uses the floating guide during the lesson for support if needed.

4. Screen-by-Screen Breakdown

4.1 Onboarding

Screen 1 — Year group - Single question: what year is your child in? (Reception / Year 1 / Year 2 / Not sure) - Used to suggest a starting phase (Reception → Phase 1–2, Year 1 → Phase 3–4, Year 2 → Phase 5–6); the suggestion is shown as a highlight, not a lock — all phases remain accessible - No name or personal details collected at this stage — a name can be added optionally later in Settings if the parent wants personalised encouragement messages

Screen 2 — Parent English level - "I'm a native English speaker" - "I speak English confidently but it's not my first language" - "I'm still learning English myself" - This affects how much pronunciation scaffolding the app surfaces by default. Can be changed in Settings.

Screen 3 — How to use this app - Brief explanation: what phonics is, why it matters, what "phases" mean - Key vocabulary introduced with audio: phoneme (a single sound), grapheme (the letter or letters that represent it), blend (joining sounds to make a word), segment (breaking a word into its sounds), tricky word (a word that doesn't follow the usual rules) - Reassurance: "You don't need to know English phonics already. The app will teach you alongside your child."

4.2 Learn

The main curriculum view. Structured around the 6 phases of Letters and Sounds.

Learn home screen - Continue card — shows current phase and lesson, one tap to resume - Quick stats — sounds learned, accuracy, stars earned - Phase list — all 6 phases, each showing completion percentage; all phases accessible from day one

Phase view - Briefing card (top of screen, open on first visit / collapsed on return): - Plain-language summary: what the phase covers and what the child will be able to do by the end - Audio button: hear all sounds introduced in this phase - Expandable "Tips for home" section — activities requiring no materials - Once collapsed, the card shows only a single "About this phase ∨" row - List of lessons below the card - Each lesson row shows: title, short description, number of sounds, completion status

4.3 Curriculum Phases

Phase 1 — Sound Awareness (pre-reading)

Mainly oral. No graphemes. Focuses on listening, rhythm and blending spoken sounds.

The 7 aspects become 7 interactive activities, not lessons. These are done with a parent present.

Aspect Activity type
1 — Environmental sounds Sound recognition game: hear a sound, choose the picture
2 — Instrumental sounds Instrument identification with audio
3 — Body percussion Rhythm-copying game (tap the pattern)
4 — Rhythm & rhyme Rhyming word pairs — match or complete
5 — Alliteration Find the odd one out (different starting sound)
6 — Voice sounds Copy mouth sounds (whisper, shout, hum)
7 — Oral blending & segmenting Hear segmented sounds → blend to find the word; hear a word → count phonemes

Parent companion for Phase 1 explains: - Why listening skills come before letters - What "oral blending" means and how to practise it in everyday life - Examples: "What does /d/-/ɒ/-/g/ say? Yes, dog!"

Phase 2 — First Letters (19 graphemes)

The starting point for letter sounds and reading first short words.

Letters are introduced in sets. Within each set, children immediately read and spell decodable words.

Set Letters Decodable words introduced
1 s, a, t, p sat, tap, pat, sap
2 i, n, m, d in, it, nip, dim, mad, dip
3 g, o, c, k got, cog, dock, mock
4 ck, e, u, r neck, duck, run, red
5 h, b, f/ff, l/ll, ss him, but, fill, miss

Tricky words for Phase 2: the, to, go, no

Phase 3 — 25 New Graphemes

Introduces digraphs (2-letter graphemes) and extends to almost all English sounds.

Sets 6–12 add: j, v, w, x, y, z/zz, qu, then digraphs ch, sh, th, ng, followed by vowel digraphs ai, ee, igh, oa, oo, ar, or, ur, ow, oi, ear, air, ure, er.

Children also learn: - Letter names (alphabet) - Reading two-syllable words - Extended tricky word list: he, she, we, me, be, was, my, you, they, her, all, are

Phase 4 — Consolidation & Longer Words

No new graphemes — focuses on reading and spelling longer words with consonant clusters.

Focus on blending and segmenting words with adjacent consonants: - CCVC: frog, drip, swap, clap - CVCC: belt, fast, damp, help - CCVCC: stomp, blend, crisp - Polysyllabic words: rocket, picnic, trumpet

Extended tricky word list: said, so, do, some, come, were, there, little, one, when, out, what

Phase 5 — Alternative Spellings & Pronunciations

Major expansion — the same sound can be spelled different ways, and the same spelling can make different sounds.

Children learn: - Alternative graphemes for the same phoneme (e.g. /eɪ/ can be spelled ai, ay, a-e, a, ey) - Alternative pronunciations of graphemes (e.g. ea = /iː/ in sea but /ɛ/ in head) - Split digraphs (magic 'e'): cake, vine, hope, cute

Extended tricky words list for Year 1 (45 words total).

This phase is particularly important for non-native parents — the irregularities of English spelling make more sense when explained through the lens of alternative graphemes.

Phase 6 — Fluency & Spelling

Reading becomes automatic; focus shifts to spelling rules, suffixes and reading longer texts.

Focus shifts to: - Reading with increasing fluency and speed - Spelling rules: adding suffixes (-s, -es, -ing, -ed, -er, -est), doubling consonants, dropping silent 'e' - Past tense - Homophones - 300 high-frequency words to read automatically

4.4 Lesson Flow

Every lesson within Phases 2–5 follows this screen sequence for each phoneme or grapheme:

[1] Introduction
[2] Hear It
[3] Example Words
[4] Blending Practice
[5] Tricky Words (if applicable)
[6] Mini Game
[7] Complete

Each step shows a small 📖 floating button in the corner. Tapping it opens a bottom sheet with parent guidance for that specific step — without leaving the lesson. The child's content stays visible behind the sheet.

[1] Introduction - Large grapheme displayed centre screen - Phoneme written in IPA (e.g. /ʃ/ for sh) - Mnemonic image (e.g. finger to lips for sh) - 📖 sheet: "This is the /ʃ/ sound, written as sh. It's like the sound you make to ask someone to be quiet."

[2] Hear It - Large tap-to-play button - Animated mouth showing articulation - Slow/normal audio speed toggle - IPA notation with a plain-English explanation: "Your lips come together and air pushes through — no voice" - 📖 sheet: "Listen carefully. Then try to say it yourself before playing it to your child."

[3] Example Words - 3–4 words containing the target grapheme - Each word shown with the target grapheme highlighted - Tap each word to hear it spoken - Image alongside each word - 📖 sheet: pronunciation tip for any words non-native speakers commonly mispronounce

[4] Blending Practice - Words broken into phoneme segments: sh · i · p - Tap each segment to hear the sound - Tap the full word to hear it blended - Arrows animate left-to-right to show blending direction - 📖 sheet: "Help your child sweep their finger under each sound as they say it, then run it smoothly under the whole word"

[5] Tricky Words (if the lesson introduces any) - Card per tricky word - Audio for each - Visual highlight showing which part follows the rules and which doesn't - 📖 sheet: plain explanation — e.g. "The word said looks like it should say /seɪd/ but it says /sɛd/. Just learn it by sight."

[6] Mini Game - Rotating set of game types (see Section 4.6) - Difficulty scales to the child's recent accuracy - 📖 sheet: what skill the game is practising and how to help if the child gets stuck

[7] Complete - Celebration animation, star earned - Summary: "You learned the /ʃ/ sound today" - 📖 sheet: one or two suggested home activities to try before the next lesson

4.5 Practice

Adaptive drilling section, separate from the curriculum flow. Suitable for solo child use after a parent has introduced the content.

Recommended — app selects 3 sounds with lowest recent accuracy for a quick 5-minute session.

Weak sounds — list of all sounds below a configurable accuracy threshold (default 75%), each showing a percentage.

Custom practice — parent or child selects any learned sounds to drill.

Drill types (rotated)

Drill What it tests
Sound → Grapheme Hear a sound, choose the correct letter(s) from 4 options
Grapheme → Sound See a letter/digraph, tap the button that makes the right sound
Blend the word See a word broken into segments, blend and choose the correct image
Segment the word Hear a word, tap the correct number of phonemes, then place each one
Tricky word flash See a word for 2 seconds, say it aloud, tap to hear correct answer
Sound in word Hear a word, identify which position the target sound is in (start / middle / end)

4.6 Play

Standalone games that reinforce learned sounds without following the lesson sequence. All games are available from the start — the parent chooses what's appropriate for their child.

Game Mechanic
Sound Hunt Hear a target sound, tap all pictures whose names contain it
Build the Word Letter tiles scattered on screen, drag them into the correct order
Tricky Word Challenge Flashcard speed game — say the word before the timer runs out
Blending Race Phoneme sounds play one by one; blend and tap the correct word
Rhyme Finder Shown a word, choose all rhyming words from a set
Sound Snap Card flip game — match grapheme cards to picture cards
Spelling Bee Hear a word, spell it by selecting letter tiles (segmenting)
Sentence Builder Arrange word cards to form a given caption

Each game lets the parent choose which sounds or phase to practise — so the content is targeted even if the game itself is always accessible. Games track a personal best (accuracy or time). No internet leaderboards — this is a family app.

4.7 Library

Reference section, primarily for parents. Browsable, not gated by progress.

Sound Chart

  • All ~44 phonemes in a grid
  • Each cell: grapheme(s), IPA symbol, tap to hear
  • Filter by phase
  • Each cell shows: "When your child will learn this" and common spellings of the same sound

Word Bank

  • All words introduced in lessons, filterable by:
  • Phase
  • Specific sound
  • Word type (decodable / tricky)
  • Each word: tap to hear, see which sounds it contains highlighted
  • Parents can mark words their child has mastered or is struggling with

Tricky Words List

  • All tricky words organised by phase
  • Each word: audio, which part is irregular, memory tip
  • Mastery tracker (learned / in progress / not started)

Parent Guides

One guide per phase. Written in plain English. Each guide contains:

  1. What your child is learning — plain summary of phase objectives
  2. Key vocabulary — terms used in the app, explained simply
  3. Sounds guide — audio for every sound in the phase, with:
  4. How to make the sound (mouth position described)
  5. Common mispronunciations by non-native speakers (e.g. /v/ vs /w/, /θ/ vs /d/, /ɹ/ vs /r/)
  6. IPA symbol
  7. Home practice ideas — 5 activities needing no materials
  8. What to say — scripts for common teaching moments (e.g. "Sweep your finger under the letters as we say each sound…")
  9. Signs of progress — what to look for before moving to the next phase
  10. Common questions — FAQ for parents (Why is 'the' a tricky word? What if my child skips a sound? Is my accent a problem?)

Glossary

Alphabetical list of every phonics term used in the app, each with: - Plain-English definition - Example - Audio pronunciation of the term itself

4.8 Progress

Child view (visible to child)

  • Star count and streak
  • Phase progress bars
  • Badges / achievements unlocked
  • Recent activity ("You learned 3 new sounds this week!")

Parent dashboard

  • Per-phase completion and accuracy breakdown
  • Accuracy per individual sound — colour-coded (green / amber / red)
  • Time spent per week (chart)
  • Tricky words: mastered vs in progress
  • Suggested next focus areas
  • Weekly summary (shareable, e.g. to show a teacher)

6. Key Features for Non-Native Speaking Parents

These features are the most important differentiators from other phonics apps, which typically assume the parent is a native English speaker.

6.1 Parent Pronunciation Studio

A dedicated screen within the Parent Guide for each phase where the parent can: - Hear every sound in the phase - See animated mouth position - Record themselves and play back (to practise before teaching their child) - See contrast pairs: sounds that are often confused by speakers of specific language backgrounds (e.g. /v/ vs /w/ for many European speakers, /θ/ and /ð/ for almost everyone, /æ/ vs /ɛ/ for many)

6.2 "Before you teach" prompts

Before starting a new lesson, the app shows the parent a 30-second summary: - "Today your child will learn the /ʃ/ sound, written as sh" - Audio button: "Tap to hear what it sounds like" - "Tip: make sure you can say this sound before the lesson"

6.3 Transparent IPA

Every phoneme in the app shows its IPA symbol alongside plain-English description. Parents who are learning English themselves may find IPA more reliable than relying on their own mental model of a sound.

6.4 Teaching scripts

The 📖 floating button during lesson flow surfaces exact suggested phrasing the parent can say aloud. For example:

"Look at these two letters together: s and h. When we see them together, they make one sound: /ʃ/. Listen — [tap audio]. Can you say it? Good. Now let's find that sound in some words."

Scripts are written in simple English and can be translated via in-app language settings (display language — the audio content remains in English).

6.5 Glossary always accessible

A persistent "?" icon appears next to every piece of phonics terminology throughout the app. Tapping it opens a plain-English explanation in a bottom sheet, with audio pronunciation of the term.

6.6 Accent reassurance

The app explicitly addresses accent anxiety in the Parent Guide introduction:

"Your accent doesn't matter. What matters is that your child hears the sounds clearly. Use the app's audio as your model — listen to it yourself first, then play it together. You don't need to sound like the audio; you just need to help your child notice the sounds."

7. Navigation Structure

Bottom tab bar (always visible in app)
├── Learn     (book icon)
├── Practice  (target icon)
├── Play      (game controller icon)
├── Library   (bookmark icon)
└── Progress  (chart icon)

Top bar
├── Left:  Phase/lesson breadcrumb (or back arrow in lesson flow)
├── Centre: App logo (taps to home)
└── Right: Settings

Lesson flow uses a full-screen modal with its own back button and progress bar — the main tab bar is hidden during active lessons to reduce distraction.

9. Out of Scope (for v1)

  • Platforms: Android and iOS only. No web, macOS, Windows or Linux builds.
  • Handwriting / letter formation exercises (the framework covers this but it needs a separate pencil-control interaction model)
  • Classroom / multi-child mode
  • Teacher reporting
  • Curriculum alignment reports for specific UK schools
  • Online sync / cloud backup (can be added later)
  • Languages other than English for the parent UI (architecture supports it, not in v1)
